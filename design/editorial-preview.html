The 2026 Iran War

How 112 days of conflict, a closed Strait of Hormuz, and 22,500 stranded mariners rewrote global energy markets — and what it meant for India.

By Sahasrik Ragani  ·  Ahmedabad University  ·  28 Feb – 20 Jun 2026

$166.96
Oman crude peak · /bbl · all-time high, 19 Mar 2026

For roughly one hundred of the conflict’s one hundred and twelve days, the single most important artery in the global oil trade went dark. This is an accounting of what that cost — measured in barrels, in rupees, and in people.

On the morning of 2 March 2026, the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps declared the Strait of Hormuz closed to commercial traffic. Within seventy-two hours the Oman/Dubai Mercantile Exchange marker — the benchmark that prices the crude already moving east of the blockade — had detached from every other grade in the world.1 By 19 March it printed $166.96 a barrel, an all-time high, on a single Chinese panic bid.

The Reserve Bank of India sold roughly twenty billion dollars in one week to defend the rupee.2 It was not enough. The currency kept falling through May, and the current-account arithmetic that underwrites the entire Indian growth story began, quietly, to break.

“Every ten dollars a barrel, sustained, widens India’s current-account deficit by about fifty basis points. The Strait did not need to stay shut for long to do lasting damage.”

Morgan Stanley India · Oil Sensitivity Model

What follows is the record: the market data, the shipping data, and the macroeconomic data, each tied to a dated institutional source. No interpretation is offered where the numbers speak plainly.
